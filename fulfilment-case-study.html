Order Fulfilment Improvement · Work experience at P.N. Igweh Merchants · 2020–2023

How can earlier stock checks make deliveries more reliable?

Branch and logistics work exposed gaps between recorded stock, order handling and delivery planning. I gathered operational needs from managers, suppliers and frontline teams, supported reconciliation and workflow changes, and tracked the resulting performance.

Results are based on internal operational tracking during the period. The maps, rules and user stories on this page reconstruct the approach for a portfolio review; they are illustrative and were not signed-off project artefacts.

Problem and stakeholders

Stock discrepancies made fulfilment less predictable. Delays at picking and dispatch affected customer commitments while routing choices added costs.

StakeholderNeed
Branch managerReliable stock and delivery KPIs for decisions.
Warehouse teamClear item availability and consistent picking steps.
Drivers and logistics teamConfirmed orders and practical delivery plans.
Suppliers and customersTimely exception reporting and realistic commitments.

Process view

Earlier process · reconstructed

Order received → stock record checked → physical availability confirmed during picking → discrepancy handled → dispatch arranged → delivery status reported.

Potential delay points: late discovery of stock differences, order rework and ad hoc route coordination.

Improved process · reconstructed

Order received → stock status reconciled → exceptions flagged before picking → pick and dispatch checks recorded → routes coordinated → delivery and inventory KPIs reviewed.

Operational objective: detect exceptions earlier and make handoffs visible.

Requirements and acceptance checks

Illustrative needAcceptance check
As a branch manager, I need a regular discrepancy report so I can prioritise stock checks.Report shows SKU, expected quantity, counted quantity, variance and resolution owner.
As a warehouse colleague, I need exceptions flagged before picking so I can avoid incomplete orders.Orders with an unresolved stock mismatch are identified before dispatch.
As a logistics coordinator, I need confirmed orders and destinations before assigning a route.Dispatch list includes delivery location, order status and assigned driver.

Root-cause hypotheses

The reconstructed process suggests three issues to test: delayed stock reconciliation, inconsistent picking-to-dispatch handoffs, and delivery planning before order status is confirmed. These are hypotheses for review, rather than documented findings from a formal root-cause investigation.

Proposed business rules

  • Record each stock variance against an item, count date and responsible owner.
  • Resolve or explicitly authorise stock exceptions before confirming dispatch.
  • Assign a driver only after order status and destination are confirmed.
  • Record delivery exceptions with a reason and follow-up owner.

KPI definitions for a future baseline

Use a consistent comparison period and order mix. Track inventory variance rate (items with a discrepancy ÷ items counted), median time from order receipt to dispatch, on-time deliveries ÷ completed deliveries, and logistics cost per completed delivery. These proposed definitions do not reconstruct the denominators behind the historical results.

Measures and reported outcomes

These figures come from the branch’s internal tracking while I worked there, January 2020 to October 2023. Each one is a relative change against the level before the workflow changes, not a percentage-point change. For example, a 12% improvement in on-time delivery means the on-time rate rose by 12% of its starting value. The starting values are internal and are not published here.

What I would validate in a formal project

I would baseline each KPI with a defined time window and denominator, retain signed-off as-is and to-be maps, document exception volumes, and compare performance before and after rollout while controlling for order mix and seasonality.
